# Voice and Style Guide

## Density

Every sentence must carry a new claim. Do not restate. Do not summarize what was just said. If a paragraph can be cut without losing meaning, cut it.

## No Hedging

Forbidden words: arguably, perhaps, likely, seems, might, could be considered, to some extent, it is possible that.

If a claim is uncertain, say why. If it is certain, say it directly. Do not soften the edge.

## Opinionated

Take a position. The reader is a senior developer who has read enough neutral prose. State what you believe and defend it. Passive constructions are lazy — name the agent.

## Examples

- Weak: "It could be argued that verification does not escape the constraint."
- Strong: "Verification does not escape the constraint."

- Weak: "Scaling may redistribute failures without eliminating them."
- Strong: "Scaling redistributes failures. It does not eliminate them."

## Sentence Rhythm

Use short sentences for claims. Use longer sentences only when the logical structure demands it. Break lists into parallel constructions. Avoid nested clauses.

## Technical Tone

Write as if explaining to a colleague who is competent but not yet convinced. Assume the reader can follow formal notation. Do not define basic terms. Do not apologize for the difficulty.

## Forbidden Phrases

- "In this section, we will..."
- "It is important to note that..."
- "As mentioned earlier..."
- "This begs the question..." (misused)
